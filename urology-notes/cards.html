Emergency

Tachycardia after surgery

Page

RN: Mr. D., bed 7, POD 2 after radical cystectomy with ileal conduit. HR 128, BP 104/70.

Danger signs

  • Low BP, cool extremities, or a narrow pulse pressure suggest bleeding. HR often rises before BP falls.
  • New low SpO2, dyspnea or chest pain: consider PE or pneumothorax.
  • Fever or rigors with low BP or new confusion: possible sepsis.

Key decision

IfBP low, SpO2 falling, or new confusion → rapid response and senior now
IfECG not sinus (irregular or wide QRS) → treat the rhythm with your senior
Ifsinus and stable → work up bleeding, sepsis, leak, PE, hypovolemia and pain

Call the senior

S
Calling from urology about Mr. D. in bed 7. He has HR 128, up from the 90s, and BP 104/70.
B
POD 2 after radical cystectomy with ileal conduit. SpO2 95% on room air. Temp 38.1 °C.
A
Sinus tachycardia on ECG. Abdomen more tender, drain output up. Concern for a leak or sepsis; bleeding not excluded.
R
Please come to see him. OK to send cultures, lactate and drain creatinine now?

Emergency

Hypotension on the night of surgery

Page

RN: Mr. H., bed 4, POD 0 after open left nephrectomy, epidural running. BP 84/50, HR 112. He feels dizzy.

Danger signs

  • SBP under 90 or MAP under 65.
  • More serious with confusion, cold clammy skin or low urine output.
  • Rising HR, falling Hgb, or more or darker drain blood.
  • New abdominal, flank or back pain.
  • Fever, chest pain, dyspnea, low SpO2, or urticaria and wheeze.
  • Low BP, low SpO2 and reduced or absent breath sounds on one side: consider tension pneumothorax.

Key decision

Ifcold skin, rising HR, falling Hgb or more drain blood → treat as bleeding. Senior now, and get blood ready
Iffever or warm skin → sepsis. Send cultures. Antibiotics within 1 hour if shock is possible or sepsis is likely
Ifurticaria or wheeze after a new drug or blood → anaphylaxis. Stop the drug or blood, give IM epinephrine and call rapid response
Iflow SpO2, dyspnea and reduced or absent breath sounds on one side → possible tension pneumothorax. Call rapid response and your senior now. Do not wait for an X-ray

Call the senior

S
This is urology, about Mr. H., bed 4, POD 0 open left nephrectomy. BP 84/50, HR 112, was 128/76 at 4 pm.
B
Epidural running, on heparin prophylaxis.
A
Cool and pale, left flank tender, drain darker; concern for bleeding. Rapid response team at bedside; they started crystalloid. Labs and type and crossmatch sent.
R
Please come to the bedside now.

Emergency

Infected obstructing stone

Page

ED: Mr. R., 58, left ureteral stone on CT, temp 39.0 °C, HR 118, BP 98/60. Requesting a urology consult.

Danger signs

  • Suspect an infected, obstructed kidney if a stone comes with any of these:
    • Fever 37.8 °C (100 °F) or higher
    • WBC over 15,000
    • Pyuria
    • Hypotension
    • Unexplained tachycardia
  • Systolic BP under 90 or new confusion means likely sepsis and possible shock. Give antibiotics within 1 hour.
  • He can look stable, then become critically ill within hours.
    • Risk is higher with older age, diabetes, a solitary kidney or immunosuppression.

Key decision

Ifobstructed kidney with signs of infection → urgent drainage (stent or nephrostomy tube) plus antibiotics.
Iftoo unstable for general anesthesia, or the obstruction is severe → nephrostomy tube often preferred varies by site.
Ifanticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs cannot be stopped, or severe obesity → a ureteral stent may be needed.

Call the senior

S
Urology consult in the ED for Mr. R., 58, with a left proximal ureteral stone and fever.
R
He needs urgent drainage, so please see him now. Stent, or IR nephrostomy tube? Also, which analgesic do you want? NSAIDs are not safe for him.

Emergency

Testicular torsion

Page

ED: Mr. L., 19, sudden left testicular pain for 3 hours, vomited twice. The ED asks for a urology consult.

Danger signs

  • Sudden one-sided scrotal pain, sometimes with nausea or vomiting, suggests torsion.
  • Torsion is a surgical emergency. The chance of saving the testis drops after 6 hours of pain.
  • Testes saved, by hours of pain: 97% at 0 to 6 hours, 79% at 7 to 12 hours, 61% at 13 to 18 hours, 43% at 19 to 24 hours, 24% at 25 to 48 hours, 7% over 48 hours.
  • Pain felt only in the lower abdomen or groin can still be torsion. Examine the scrotum.

Key decision

Ifsuspicion is high (sudden pain, hard or high testis, vomiting) → call your senior now. OR without ultrasound.
Ifsuspicion is low or unclear → urgent Doppler, then decide with your senior.
Ifthe OR is delayed and pain is under 6 hours → manual detorsion with your senior varies by site, followed by surgery.

Call the senior

S
This is urology, in the ED with Mr. L., 19, possible left testicular torsion.
B
Sudden left scrotal pain 3 hours ago, vomited twice.
A
Left testis high and hard, no cremasteric reflex. High suspicion.
R
Please review him now. He is NPO and labs are sent.

ED, emergency department; NPO, nothing by mouth; OR, operating room.

Emergency

Priapism

Page

ED: Mr. B., 24, with sickle cell disease. Painful erection since 3 am, now 6 hours.

Danger signs

  • A rigid, tender erection lasting 4 hours or more, with a soft glans, suggests ischemic priapism. This is an emergency.
  • Corporal blood gas in ischemic priapism: acidic (low pH), pO2 under 30, pCO2 over 60 mm Hg. Blood is dark, close to black.
  • After 24 hours, up to 90% lose normal erections.

Key decision

Ifpainful, rigid, soft glans, dark blood → ischemic: aspiration, irrigation, phenylephrine now, with your senior.
Ifpainless, partly rigid, bright red blood, often after trauma → non-ischemic: observe, ice, perineal pressure.
Ifaspiration, irrigation and phenylephrine fail → your senior plans a distal shunt in the OR.

Call the senior

S
The urology team here, in the ED with Mr. B., painful erection for 6 hours.
B
Sickle cell disease, no trauma, no erectile dysfunction drugs.
A
Rigid, tender shaft, soft glans. Looks ischemic. CBC, reticulocyte count, type and screen sent.
R
Please come to the bedside now for aspiration and phenylephrine.

ED, emergency department; OR, operating room; CBC, complete blood count; pO2, partial pressure of oxygen; pCO2, partial pressure of carbon dioxide.

Emergency

Fournier gangrene

Page

ED: Mr. G., 61, with diabetes. Scrotal pain and swelling for 2 days, now crepitus. HR 124, BP 94/60. Urgent urology consult.

Danger signs

  • Pain out of proportion to the skin findings.
  • Crepitus, bullae, dusky or black skin, or a foul smell.
  • Fever, tachycardia, tachypnea, hypotension or organ failure are late signs of sepsis or shock.

Key decision

Ifthe signs are clear, or he is unstable or septic → OR now with your senior. Do not wait for CT or lab results.
Ifhe is stable and the picture is unclear → CT can help, with your senior. It must not delay surgery.
Ifstill in doubt → surgical exploration is safer than waiting.

A
Swollen, dusky scrotum, crepitus into the perineum. Temp 38.9 °C, HR 124, BP 94/60. Labs and blood cultures sent. ED starting fluids and broad-spectrum antibiotics.
R
Urgent bedside review. He likely needs the OR.

Emergency

Hyponatremia after TURP

Page

RN: Mr. K., 2 hours after monopolar TURP. New confusion, nausea, says his vision is blurry. BP 92/50, HR 54.

Danger signs

  • Seizure, marked drowsiness, coma or shock: severe hyponatremia.
  • New confusion or vision change after a monopolar case. Send a stat sodium even for nausea alone.
  • Low BP with bradycardia can be TUR syndrome or bladder perforation. Check for both.
  • Short of breath, crackles or low SpO2: fluid overload with pulmonary edema.

Key decision

Ifhyponatremia with seizure, marked drowsiness or delirium → senior at the bedside now. The senior decides on hypertonic saline varies by site
Ifmilder symptoms only (nausea, fatigue) and fully alert → senior chooses furosemide with saline or slow hypertonic saline varies by site
Ifabdominal distension, abdominal pain or shoulder pain → suspect perforation at any sodium level. Cystogram with the senior

Call the senior

S
Urology here, at the bedside of Mr. K. He is 2 hours after monopolar TURP and has new confusion, nausea and blurry vision. BP 92/50, HR 54. Rapid response team called.
B
Long resection with glycine irrigation. Pre-op sodium normal.
A
Possible TUR syndrome. On a cardiac monitor; ECG done. Lungs clear, SpO2 96% on room air. Abdomen soft, no shoulder pain. Perforation not ruled out. CBI switched to saline. Stat sodium and osmolality sent.
R
Please see him at the bedside now. Need the sodium plan, level of care, and a decision on holding the CBI.

Low urine output after surgery

Page

RN: Mr. S., POD 1 after robotic partial nephrectomy. The Foley put out 40 mL in 3 hours.

Danger signs

  • Rising HR, falling BP, cool skin or falling Hgb suggest bleeding. HR often rises before BP falls.
  • No urine at all points to a blocked Foley, another obstruction, or renal vascular occlusion.
  • Fever, tachycardia, abdominal distension or low BP: consider urine leak or sepsis.
  • Severe hyperkalemia is also an emergency.

Key decision

Iffull bladder on scan, or clot or mucus on flush → catheter problem. Restore drainage first.
IfHR up, BP down, or Hgb falling → manage as bleeding and call your senior now.
Ifcatheter drains, vitals stable, drain output rising → suspect a urine leak. Send drain fluid creatinine.

B
HR 104, BP 112/70, no fever. Foley flushes clear, bladder scan empty, drain output not rising.
A
No catheter problem and no sign of a leak. Concerned about bleeding or hypovolemia. Labs sent.
R
Can you see him now? Need a decision on fluids or a CT.

Common call

Fever after surgery, by day

Page

RN: Mr. W., POD 1 after left PCNL. Temp 39.0 °C (102.2 °F), HR 118, shaking chills.

Danger signs

  • Fever with rigors, low BP, tachycardia or new confusion suggests sepsis. See him and start the workup now.
  • Organ dysfunction: SBP under 90, MAP under 65, lactate over 2 mmol/L, low urine output.
    • With fever, any of these suggests sepsis.
  • POD 0 to 1, temp over 40 °C (104 °F) with rigidity or acidosis: consider malignant hyperthermia.
    • Call anesthesia and your senior now.
  • Fever with severe wound pain on POD 0 to 1 raises concern for gas gangrene.
  • After PCNL, fever with low SpO2 or dyspnea can come from hydrothorax or pneumothorax as well as from sepsis.

Key decision

Iflow BP, possible shock, or sepsis likely → call your senior now. Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour
Ifpossible sepsis, no shock → see him and call your senior. Antibiotics within 3 hours if concern persists
Ifstable, low-grade, first 48 to 72 hours, no source, no stone surgery or surgery on an infected urinary tract → observe and recheck. Do not start antibiotics for the fever alone

Call the senior

S
Urology, calling about Mr. W., POD 1 after left PCNL. He has temp 39.0 °C, HR 118, BP 94/58 and rigors.
R
Please come to the bedside now. Start broad-spectrum antibiotics within the hour?

Danger signs

  • Tense abdomen or shoulder pain, with low BP and bradycardia.

Common call

Difficult Foley placement

Page

RN: Mr. C., 74, in urinary retention. The Foley would not pass after two tries. Can someone from urology help?

Danger signs

  • Blood at the meatus or pelvic trauma can mean urethral injury. No blind attempt.
  • Catheter kinks and bloody discharge after tries: possible urethral perforation. Call your senior.
  • Retention that cannot be drained is urgent. Do not keep retrying.

Key decision

Ifblood at the meatus, or recent prostatectomy or urethral surgery → stop; call your senior.
Ifthe coudé passes to the hub and urine flows → inflate the balloon slowly.
Ifthe coudé fails → stop and call your senior for a 12 Fr silicone, guidewire or cystoscopy.

B
Prior TURP. Two 16 Fr straight tries, then one gentle coudé try; no blood.
A
Bladder full and tender, vitals stable. Possible bladder neck contracture or stricture.
R
Could you come and try a 12 Fr silicone, a guidewire or flexible cystoscopy?

RN, registered nurse; Fr, French, the catheter size unit; TURP, transurethral resection of the prostate.

Common call

Urinary retention consult from the ED, then the voiding trial

Page

ED: Mr. T., 78, has not voided since last night. Suprapubic pain, bladder scan about 900 mL. Consult for urinary retention.

Danger signs

  • Retention with fever, confusion, or low BP that does not recover: possible urosepsis. Start the sepsis workup now.
  • New back pain, saddle numbness, leg weakness or bowel change raise concern for cauda equina syndrome. Urgent MRI and spine team.

Key decision

IfFoley in, no fever, creatinine at baseline, 1500 mL or less drained → home with the Foley (senior OK), alpha-blocker, voiding trial in about 3 days varies by site
Ifsepsis, AKI, electrolyte problem or over 1500 mL drained → admit (discuss with your senior) varies by site
Ifthe Foley does not pass → no force. Coudé only by someone trained in it, or urology varies by site. Next steps on the page "Difficult Foley placement"

A
Likely BPH. No sign of sepsis, AKI or cauda equina.

Common call

Paraphimosis

Page

RN: Mr. J. had a Foley placed this morning. His foreskin is swollen and will not go back over the glans.

Danger signs

  • Glans dark, blue, black or pale: possible ischemia or necrosis. A pink glans means the blood supply is likely intact.
  • In long-standing cases, arterial flow falls over hours to days.
  • If the nurse reports "penile swelling", see the patient in person. Paraphimosis can be painless.

Key decision

Ifthe glans is pink and the foreskin reduces → clean, document and tell your senior. He still needs urology review for a dorsal slit or circumcision
Ifreduction fails after compression → call your senior for a block, puncture or dorsal slit
Ifglans dark, blue, black or pale → call your senior at once

Call the senior

S

B
Foreskin retracted since placement, Foley draining.
A
Glans swollen but pink. Compression and manual reduction failed.
R
Can you come and do a block or dorsal slit?

RN, registered nurse.

Common call

Ileus after cystectomy

Page

RN: Mr. A. is POD 4 after radical cystectomy with ileal conduit. Nausea, vomited twice, abdomen distended, no flatus.

Danger signs

  • Fever, tachycardia, focal tenderness, high WBC or acidosis suggest strangulated bowel, which may need surgery.
  • A rigid abdomen, rebound or guarding points to peritonitis or perforation.
  • High lactate raises concern for ischemic bowel.
  • Fever after a urine leak can mean an infected urinoma.

Key decision

Ifsoft abdomen, normal vitals, no flatus yet → likely ileus. If it lasts past 72 h, look for a cause
Iffever, tachycardia, focal tenderness, rebound or rising lactate → call your senior now for CT and sepsis care
Ifdrain output is high or rising → possible urine leak. Send drain fluid for creatinine and tell your senior

B
No flatus yet. Drain output up since this morning.
A
Afebrile, HR 92, BP 128/76, no rebound. Now past 72 h, so a cause needs to be found.

Please see him. Do you agree with an NG tube, imaging and drain creatinine?

Common call

Three pages at once

Page

RN, three pages in 5 minutes: bed 9, POD 1 after nephrectomy, temp 38.4 °C. Bed 7, 81, wants something to help him sleep. Bed 3, Foley not draining for 2 hours, suprapubic pain.

Danger signs

  • Vitals past typical rapid response triggers varies by site:
    • HR over 140 or under 40. RR over 28 or under 8.
    • Systolic BP over 180 or under 90. SpO2 under 90% on oxygen.
  • New confusion or a sudden change in mental status.
  • New delirium can signal a life-threatening illness.
  • Staff concern alone, such as a worried nurse, is a rapid response trigger.

Key decision

Ifanyone meets a rapid response trigger, or the nurse is very worried → see that patient first and call the RRT or your senior now.
Ifbed 7 is newly confused → see bed 7 now, because new confusion is a danger sign.
Iftwo or more may be sick → call your senior now and agree on who sees whom.
Ifall vitals are normal, no one newly confused → see bed 3 first for the blocked Foley with pain, and phone the nurses for the other two.

A
Blocked catheter, possibly clot. Bed 9 has 38.4 °C on POD 1 with normal vitals. Bed 7 wants a sleep aid and is not confused.
