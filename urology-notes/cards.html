Emergency

Fast heart rate after surgery

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, POD 2 after radical cystectomy with ileal conduit. HR 128, BP 104/70.

Danger signs

  • Low BP, cold hands, or a narrow pulse pressure: think bleeding. Tachycardia often comes first.
  • New low SpO2, short of breath, or chest pain: think PE or pneumothorax.
  • Fever or rigors with low BP or new confusion: possible sepsis.

Key decision

IfBP low, SpO2 falling, or new confusion → rapid response and senior now
IfECG not sinus (irregular or wide QRS) → treat the rhythm with your senior
Ifsinus and stable → find the cause: bleeding, sepsis, leak, PE, low volume, pain

Call the senior

S
Urology, about bed 12. Mr. X has HR 128, up from the 90s, and BP 104/70.
B
POD 2 after radical cystectomy with ileal conduit. SpO2 95% on room air. Temp 38.1 °C.
A
Sinus tachycardia on ECG. Belly more tender, drain output up. Worried about a leak or sepsis; bleeding not excluded.
R
Please come see him. OK to send cultures, lactate and drain creatinine now?

Emergency

Low blood pressure the night of surgery

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, POD 0 after open left nephrectomy, epidural running. BP 84/50, HR 112. He feels dizzy.

Danger signs

  • SBP under 90 or MAP under 65.
  • Worse with confusion, cold clammy skin or low urine output.
  • Rising HR, falling Hgb, or more or darker drain blood.
  • New belly, flank or back pain.
  • Fever, chest pain, short of breath, low SpO2, or hives and wheeze.
  • Low BP, low SpO2, and one side of the chest quiet: think tension pneumothorax.

Key decision

Ifcold, HR up, Hgb falling or more drain blood → bleeding: senior now, blood ready
Iffever or warm skin → sepsis: cultures; antibiotics within 1 hour if shock or sepsis likely
Ifhives or wheeze after a new drug or blood → anaphylaxis: stop it, IM epinephrine, rapid response
Iflow SpO2, short of breath, one side quiet → possible tension pneumothorax: rapid response and senior now. Do not wait for an X-ray

Call the senior

S
Urology, bed 12, Mr. X, POD 0 open left nephrectomy. BP 84/50, HR 112, was 128/76 at 4 pm.
B
Epidural running, on heparin prophylaxis.
A
Cool and pale, left flank tender, drain darker; concern for bleeding. Rapid response team at bedside; they started crystalloid. Labs and type and crossmatch sent.
R
Please come now.

Emergency

Infected obstructing stone

Page

ED: Mr. X, 58, left ureteral stone on CT, temp 39.0 °C, HR 118, BP 98/60. Can urology see him?

Danger signs

  • Stone plus any of these: think infected, blocked kidney.
    • Fever 37.8 °C (100 °F) or higher
    • WBC over 15,000
    • Pyuria
    • Low BP
    • Unexplained fast heart rate
  • Systolic BP under 90 or new confusion: likely sepsis, possible shock. Antibiotics within 1 hour.
  • He can look stable, then become critically ill within hours.
    • Higher risk: older age, diabetes, solitary kidney, immunocompromised.

Key decision

Ifblocked kidney plus signs of infection → urgent drainage (stent or nephrostomy tube) plus antibiotics.
Iftoo unstable for general anesthesia, or the blockage is severe → nephrostomy tube often preferred varies by site.
Ifblood thinners cannot be stopped, or severe obesity → a ureteral stent may be needed.

Call the senior

S
Urology, ED consult, Mr. X, 58, left proximal ureteral stone with fever.
R
Please see him now for urgent drainage. Stent, or IR nephrostomy tube? Also, what pain medicine do you want? NSAIDs are not safe for him.

Emergency

Testicular torsion

Page

ED: Mr. X, 19, sudden left testicle pain for 3 hours, vomited twice. Can urology see him?

Danger signs

  • Sudden one-sided scrotal pain, sometimes with nausea or vomiting: think torsion.
  • Torsion is a true emergency. The chance of saving the testis drops after 6 hours of pain.
  • Testes kept, by hours of pain: 97% at 0 to 6 hours, 79% at 7 to 12 hours, 61% at 13 to 18 hours, 43% at 19 to 24 hours, 24% at 25 to 48 hours, 7% over 48 hours.
  • Pain only in the lower belly or groin can still be torsion. Examine the scrotum.

Key decision

Ifhigh suspicion (sudden pain, hard or high testis, vomiting) → senior now, OR without ultrasound.
Ifsuspicion is low or unclear → urgent Doppler, then decide with your senior.
Ifthe OR is delayed and pain is under 6 hours → manual detorsion with your senior varies by site, then still the OR.

Call the senior

S
Urology consult, ED bed 12, Mr. X, 19, possible left torsion.
B
Sudden left scrotal pain 3 hours ago, vomited twice.
A
Left testis high and hard, no cremasteric reflex. High suspicion.
R
Please see him now. He is NPO, labs sent.

ED, emergency department; NPO, nothing by mouth; OR, operating room.

Emergency

Priapism

Page

ED: Mr. X, 24, sickle cell disease, painful erection since 3 am, now 6 hours. Can urology see him?

Danger signs

  • Rigid, tender erection for 4 hours or more, soft glans: think ischemic priapism. A true emergency.
  • Ischemic gas: acidic (low pH), pO2 under 30, pCO2 over 60 mm Hg. Blood almost black.
  • Over 24 hours: up to 90% lose normal erections. Treat early.

Key decision

Ifpainful, rigid, soft glans, dark blood → ischemic: aspiration, irrigation, phenylephrine now, with your senior.
Ifpainless, partly rigid, bright red blood, often after trauma → non-ischemic: observe, ice, perineal pressure.
Ifaspiration, irrigation and phenylephrine fail → your senior plans a distal shunt in the OR.

Call the senior

S
Urology, ED consult, Mr. X in bed 12, painful erection for 6 hours.
B
Sickle cell disease, no trauma, no erectile dysfunction drugs.
A
Rigid, tender shaft, soft glans. Looks ischemic. CBC, reticulocyte count, type and screen sent.
R
Please come now for aspiration and phenylephrine.

ED, emergency department; OR, operating room; CBC, complete blood count; pO2, partial pressure of oxygen; pCO2, partial pressure of carbon dioxide.

Emergency

Fournier gangrene

Page

ED: Mr. X, 61, diabetic, scrotal pain and swelling for 2 days, now crepitus. HR 124, BP 94/60. Can urology see him?

Danger signs

  • Pain out of proportion to what the skin shows.
  • Crepitus, bullae, dusky or black skin, or a foul smell.
  • Fever, fast HR, fast breathing, low BP or organ failure: late signs of sepsis or shock.

Key decision

Ifclear signs, unstable or septic → OR now with your senior. Do not wait for CT or lab results.
Ifstable and unclear → CT can help, with your senior. It must not delay surgery.
Ifstill in doubt → surgical exploration is safer than waiting.

Call the senior

S
Urology, ED consult, Mr. X, 61, concern for Fournier gangrene.
B
Diabetic, 2 days of scrotal pain, worse today.
A
Swollen, dusky scrotum, crepitus into the perineum. Temp 38.9 °C, HR 124, BP 94/60. Labs and blood cultures sent. ED starting fluids and broad-spectrum antibiotics.
R
Please see him now. He likely needs the OR.

ED, emergency department; HR, heart rate; BP, blood pressure; OR, operating room; CT, computed tomography.

Emergency

Low sodium after TURP

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, 2 hours after monopolar TURP. New confusion, nausea, says his vision is blurry. BP 92/50, HR 54.

Danger signs

  • Seizure, very drowsy, coma, or shock: severe low sodium.
  • New confusion or vision change after a monopolar case. Even nausea alone: stat sodium.
  • Low BP and slow pulse: TUR syndrome or bladder perforation. Check for both.
  • Short of breath, crackles or low SpO2: fluid overload with lung edema.

Key decision

Iflow sodium with seizure, very drowsy, or delirium → senior at bedside now. Hypertonic saline per senior varies by site
Ifmilder symptoms only (nausea, tired), fully alert → senior picks: furosemide with saline, or slow hypertonic saline varies by site
Ifswollen belly, belly pain or shoulder pain → think perforation, at any sodium. Cystogram with your senior

Call the senior

S
Urology, about bed 12, Mr. X, 2 hours after monopolar TURP. New confusion, nausea, blurry vision. BP 92/50, HR 54. Rapid response team called.
B
Long resection with glycine irrigation. Pre-op sodium normal.
A
Concern for TUR syndrome. On a cardiac monitor; ECG done. Lungs clear, SpO2 96% on room air. Belly soft, no shoulder pain, perforation not ruled out. CBI switched to saline. Stat sodium and osmolality sent.
R
Please come now. Need the sodium plan, level of care, and whether to hold the CBI.

Low urine output after surgery

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, POD 1 after robotic partial nephrectomy. The Foley put out 40 mL in 3 hours.

Danger signs

  • HR up, BP down, cool skin, Hgb falling: think bleeding. Tachycardia often comes first.
  • No urine at all: think blocked Foley, other obstruction, or blocked renal vessel.
  • Fever, fast pulse, swollen belly, or low BP: think urine leak or sepsis.
  • Severe high potassium: an emergency too.

Key decision

Iffull bladder on scan, or clot or mucus on flush → catheter problem. Fix drainage first.
IfHR up, BP down, or Hgb falling → treat as bleeding. Call your senior now.
Ifcatheter drains, vitals stable, drain output rising → think urine leak. Send drain creatinine.

B
HR 104, BP 112/70, no fever. Foley flushes clear, bladder scan empty, drain output not rising.
A
Not the catheter, no sign of a leak. Worried about bleeding or low volume. Labs sent.
R
Can you see him now? Fluids or a CT?

Common call

Fever after surgery, by day

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, POD 1 after left PCNL. Temp 39.0 °C (102.2 °F), HR 118, shaking chills.

Danger signs

  • Fever with rigors, low BP, fast HR or new confusion: possible sepsis. Act now.
  • Organ dysfunction: SBP under 90, MAP under 65, lactate over 2 mmol/L, low urine output.
    • Any of these with fever: think sepsis.
  • POD 0 to 1, temp over 40 °C (104 °F) with rigidity or acidosis: think malignant hyperthermia.
    • Call anesthesia and your senior now.
  • POD 0 to 1 fever with severe wound pain: think gas gangrene.
  • After PCNL: fever with low SpO2 or short of breath. Think fluid or air in the chest (hydrothorax, pneumothorax), not only sepsis.

Key decision

Iflow BP, possible shock, or sepsis likely → call your senior now. Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour
Ifpossible sepsis, no shock → see him, call your senior. Antibiotics within 3 hours if concern persists
Ifstable, low-grade, first 48 to 72 hours, no source, no stone surgery or surgery on an infected urinary tract → watch, recheck. Do not start antibiotics for the fever alone

Call the senior

S
Urology, bed 12, Mr. X, POD 1 after left PCNL. Temp 39.0 °C, HR 118, BP 94/58, rigors.
B
Nephrostomy tube draining. Cultures and lactate sent.
A
Possible sepsis from the urinary tract.
R
Please come now. Start broad-spectrum antibiotics within the hour?

Danger signs

  • Tense belly or shoulder pain, with low BP and slow pulse.

Key decision

Ifthe op note shows a known or suspected perforation → no hand irrigation until your senior decides varies by site.

Common call

The Foley that will not go in

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, 74, in retention. Two tries, the Foley will not pass. Can urology come?

Danger signs

  • Blood at the meatus or pelvic trauma: possible urethral injury. No blind attempt.
  • Catheter kinks and bloody discharge after tries: possible urethral perforation. Call your senior.
  • Retention that nobody can drain is urgent. Do not keep trying again and again.

Key decision

Ifblood at the meatus, or recent prostatectomy or urethral surgery → stop; call your senior.
Ifthe coudé passes to the hub and urine flows → inflate the balloon slowly.
Ifthe coudé fails → stop; call your senior (12 Fr silicone, guidewire or cystoscopy).

Call the senior

S
Urology, about bed 12. Cannot place a Foley in Mr. X, 74, in retention.
B
Prior TURP. Two 16 Fr straight tries, then one gentle coudé try; no blood.
A
Bladder full and tender, vitals stable; maybe bladder neck contracture or stricture.
R
Please come for a 12 Fr silicone, guidewire or flexible cystoscopy.

RN, registered nurse; Fr, French, the catheter size unit; TURP, transurethral resection of the prostate.

Common call

Urinary retention consult from the ED, then the voiding trial

Page

ED: Mr. X, 78, unable to urinate since last night, lower belly pain, bladder scan about 900 mL. Can urology see him?

Danger signs

  • Retention plus fever, confusion, or low BP that does not recover: possible urosepsis. Start the sepsis workup now.
  • New back pain, saddle numbness, leg weakness or bowel change: think cauda equina. Urgent MRI and spine team.

Key decision

IfFoley in, no fever, creatinine at baseline, 1500 mL or less drained → home with the Foley (senior OK), alpha-blocker, voiding trial in about 3 days varies by site
Ifsepsis, AKI, electrolyte problem or over 1500 mL drained → admit (discuss with your senior) varies by site
Ifthe Foley does not pass → no force. Coudé only by someone trained in it, or urology varies by site. Next steps on the page "The Foley that will not go in"

Call the senior

S
Urology, ED consult, Mr. X, 78, acute retention.
B
Foley passed easily, 900 mL clear urine. No fever, no back or nerve signs, creatinine at baseline.
A
Likely BPH. No sign of sepsis, AKI or cauda equina.
R
Start tamsulosin, home with the Foley, voiding trial in about 3 days. OK, or do you want to see him?

Common call

Paraphimosis

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, Foley placed this morning. His foreskin is swollen and will not go back over the tip.

Danger signs

  • Glans dark, blue, black or pale: possible ischemia or necrosis. Pink: blood supply likely OK.
  • Long-standing: arterial flow drops over hours to days.
  • Phone report of "penile swelling": go and look. Never dismiss it by phone. It can be painless.

Key decision

Ifglans pink and the foreskin goes back → clean, document, tell your senior. Urology review for dorsal slit or circumcision
Ifreduction fails after compression → call your senior: block, puncture or dorsal slit
Ifglans dark, blue, black or pale → call your senior at once

Call the senior

S
Urology, about bed 12, Mr. X, paraphimosis after a Foley this morning.
B
Foreskin retracted since placement, Foley draining.
A
Glans swollen but pink. Compression and manual reduction failed.
R
Please come see him for a block or dorsal slit.

RN, registered nurse.

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, POD 4 after radical cystectomy with ileal conduit. Nausea, vomited twice, belly distended, no flatus.

Danger signs

  • Fever, fast pulse, focal tenderness, high WBC or acidosis. Think strangulated bowel. Surgery may be needed.
  • Rigid belly, rebound or guarding. Think peritonitis or perforation.
  • High lactate: think ischemic bowel.
  • Fever after a urine leak: think infected urinoma.

Key decision

Ifsoft belly, normal vitals, no flatus yet → likely ileus. Past 72 h: look for a cause
Iffever, fast pulse, focal tenderness, rebound or rising lactate → call your senior now for CT and sepsis care
Ifdrain output high or rising → drain fluid creatinine; think urine leak; tell your senior

B
No flatus yet. Drain output up since this morning.

Common call

Three pages at once

Page

RN, three pages in 5 minutes: bed 12, POD 1 after nephrectomy, temp 38.4 °C. Bed 7, 81, wants something to help him sleep. Bed 3, Foley not draining for 2 hours, lower belly pain.

Danger signs

  • Vitals past typical rapid response triggers varies by site:
    • HR over 140 or under 40. RR over 28 or under 8.
    • Systolic BP over 180 or under 90. SpO2 under 90% on oxygen.
  • New confusion or a sudden change in mental status.
  • New delirium can signal a life-threatening illness.
  • The nurse is worried. Staff concern alone is a rapid response trigger.

Key decision

Ifanyone meets a rapid response trigger, or the nurse is very worried → go there first. Call the RRT or your senior now.
Ifbed 7 is newly confused → new confusion is a danger sign. See bed 7 now.
Iftwo or more may be sick → call your senior now. Agree on who sees whom.
Ifall vitals are normal, no one newly confused → see bed 3 first: blocked Foley with pain. Phone the nurses for the other two.

Call the senior

S
Urology, three pages at once. Bed 3 is the main worry.
B
Foley not draining for 2 hours, lower belly pain, full bladder. Vitals normal. Flushing did not clear it.
A
Blocked catheter, maybe clot. Bed 12: 38.4 °C on POD 1, normal vitals. Bed 7 wants a sleep aid, not confused.
R
Please come to bed 3 now, or say which patient to see first.

RN, registered nurse; POD, postoperative day; SpO2, oxygen saturation; RRT, rapid response team; HR, heart rate; RR, respiratory rate; BP, blood pressure.
