Three pages at once

Common callSasha ZhuReviewed, version 0.1Last checked 2026-10-11Pocket card

Page

RN, three pages in 5 minutes: bed 12, POD 1 after nephrectomy, temp 38.4 °C. Bed 7, 81, wants something to help him sleep. Bed 3, Foley not draining for 2 hours, lower belly pain.

Abbreviations: RN, registered nurse; POD, postoperative day; SpO2, oxygen saturation; RRT, rapid response team; HR, heart rate; RR, respiratory rate; BP, blood pressure; TURP, transurethral resection of the prostate; IV, intravenous; CBC, complete blood count; SBAR, situation, background, assessment, request.

#Danger signs

  • New confusion or a sudden change in mental status.1
  • New delirium can signal a life-threatening illness.2
  • The nurse is worried. Staff concern alone is a rapid response trigger.1

#First steps

  1. Ask each nurse for full vitals and mental status. Ask: "Are you worried?"1
  2. Sort sick or not sick. See the possibly unstable patient first.1,3
  3. For the others: ask questions by phone, and give a time you will come. The nurse reads back any phone order.4
  4. One patient is unstable, or more than one may be sick: call your senior now. Any staff member may call the rapid response team.1,4,5
  5. Go back and recheck each patient. Tell each nurse the plan; confirm it was heard.4

#Key decision

#Do not miss

  1. Sepsis behind the fever: low BP, fast breathing, new confusion.3
  2. Blocked catheter with a filling bladder: pain, overdistension, clot retention.6,7
  3. "Can't sleep" can be delirium. Pain and a full bladder can trigger it. Quiet (hypoactive) delirium is often missed.2

#Orders and tests

Bed 3, Foley not draining, belly pain

  • Check the tubing for kinks and closed connections.6,7
  • Feel for a full, tender bladder. Bladder scan if available varies by site.6,8
  • Flush the Foley with saline and a catheter-tip syringe varies by site.6
  • This flushing advice comes from a UK hospital protocol for 3-way catheters.6
  • After prostatectomy or bladder repair, ask your senior before you flush varies by site. A neobladder catheter is different: it is flushed gently with saline on the team's schedule, and whenever it stops draining or mucus seems to block it varies by site. Never force. Call if nothing comes back.9,10,11,12
  • Dark urine or clots: see the page "Hematuria and clot retention".6
  • Still blocked after flushing: catheter change, with your senior.6
  • After TURP, a straight catheter can make a false passage.13

Bed 12, fever on POD 1

  • Full vitals and mental status.3
  • Look at lungs, wound, IV sites, Foley, belly and legs.3
  • Normal vitals, no source: early fever is often inflammatory. Recheck.3
  • See the page "Fever after surgery, by day".
  • Sepsis possible: CBC, lactate, 2 sets of blood cultures before antibiotics, urine culture.3,14
  • Chest X-ray if lung signs.3
  • Fluids if sepsis: crystalloid first. Your senior sets the volume.14
  • Antipyretic only after you check with your senior or the surgical team varies by site.3

Bed 7, asks for a sleep aid

  • Ask the nurse: new confusion? Pain? Full bladder? Treat the cause first.2
  • Non-drug first: quiet room, fewer night disturbances.2,15
  • Glasses and hearing aids by day.2
  • Older adult: avoid benzodiazepines, zolpidem-type drugs and sedating antihistamines (Beers criteria).15,16,17
  • Any sleep medicine: with your senior or per the local order set varies by site.18,19,20
  • Add fall precautions.21

#Call the senior

Situation
Urology, three pages at once. Bed 3 is the main worry.
Background
Foley not draining for 2 hours, lower belly pain, full bladder. Vitals normal. Flushing did not clear it.
Assessment
Blocked catheter, maybe clot. Bed 12: 38.4 °C on POD 1, normal vitals. Bed 7 wants a sleep aid, not confused.
Request
Please come to bed 3 now, or say which patient to see first.

#Follow up

  • Tell each nurse the plan and when you will come.
  • Recheck each one. Bed 3: bag filling, pain gone.
  • Bed 12: vitals rechecked, cultures sent. Bed 7: asleep, not confused.
  • Set the recheck time with the nurse varies by site.
  • Ask the nurse to page again for low BP, rising HR, fast breathing or new confusion.1,6
  • Or if the bag stops filling.6
  • Tell your senior about any patient who got worse. Ask who calls the attending varies by site.5
  • Document each page: time, vitals, exam, orders, who you told.
  • Hand off what is still open: stable, watcher or unstable. Add a to-do list with owner and time.4

#Normal or warning sign

Typical at many centers:

Expected
Fever in the first 1 to 2 days after surgery, normal vitals, no source. Often inflammatory.3
Expected
One flush may be enough, and the bag fills again.6
Not expected
Fever with low BP, fast breathing or new confusion. Treat as possible sepsis.1,3
Not expected
A "sleep" request from a patient who is newly confused, agitated or seeing things. Think delirium.2
Not expected
The Foley still blocked after flushing, or the urine getting darker. Call your senior.6

#Avoid

  • Don't give an older patient a benzodiazepine or zolpidem-type drug as a routine sleep aid. They raise the risk of delirium, falls and fractures.16,21
  • Don't start antibiotics for fever alone when infection is unlikely and there is no shock. Monitor closely instead.3,14
  • After TURP, don't push a new catheter in against resistance. Don't change it on your own without a guide.6,13

#Study questions

Two of the three patients sound sick. What do you do?

Call the senior now and give both in SBAR. Teams expect members to ask for help. Any staff member may call the rapid response team.1,4

Why not just give the 81-year-old a sleeping pill?

The Beers criteria advise avoiding benzodiazepines and zolpidem-type drugs in older adults. They raise delirium, fall and fracture risk. Poor sleep can be a sign of delirium. Pain and a full bladder can trigger it.2,16,21

POD 1 fever, normal vitals, no source. Antibiotics?

Usually no. Early fever is often inflammatory. Infection unlikely, no shock: defer antibiotics, monitor closely.3,14

How do you make sure the phone orders were heard right?

Check-back: the nurse repeats the order, and you confirm "correct".4
