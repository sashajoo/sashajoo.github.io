Low sodium after TURP

EmergencySasha ZhuReviewed, version 0.1Last checked 2026-10-11Pocket card

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, 2 hours after monopolar TURP. New confusion, nausea, says his vision is blurry. BP 92/50, HR 54.

Abbreviations: TURP, transurethral resection of the prostate; RN, registered nurse; BP, blood pressure; HR, heart rate; TUR, transurethral resection; CBI, continuous bladder irrigation; BMP, basic metabolic panel; OR, operating room; CBC, complete blood count; I/O, intake and output; PACU, post-anesthesia care unit; ICU, intensive care unit; SpO2, oxygen saturation; ECG, electrocardiogram.

#Danger signs

  • Seizure, very drowsy, coma, or shock: severe low sodium.1,2
  • New confusion or vision change after a monopolar case. Even nausea alone: stat sodium.1
  • Low BP and slow pulse: TUR syndrome or bladder perforation. Check for both.1,3
  • Short of breath, crackles or low SpO2: fluid overload with lung edema.3,4,5

#First steps

  1. See him now. New confusion is a rapid response criterion: call rapid response and your senior now. Your senior sets the treatment plan varies by site.6
  2. Vitals, SpO2, mental status, volume status, lungs, belly exam. Crackles, fast breathing or low SpO2: think fluid overload.1,2,4,5
  3. Cardiac monitor varies by site and 12-lead ECG. TUR syndrome can slow the pulse or cause an arrhythmia.3,4,7
  4. Check the irrigation bag. Switch any non-saline fluid to normal saline, with your senior varies by site. Bleeding: keep CBI on normal saline. Perforation concern: ask your senior about holding the CBI varies by site.1,8
  5. Stat sodium (BMP) and serum osmolality. Read the op note: monopolar or bipolar, irrigation fluid, resection time, bleeding, perforation.1,2

#Key decision

#Do not miss

  1. Severe low sodium with brain swelling: seizure, coma.2
  2. Fluid overload with pulmonary edema: short of breath, low SpO2.3,4,5
  3. Missed bladder perforation: swollen belly, slow pulse, low BP, belly or shoulder pain.1
  4. Heavy bleeding. Severe bleeding usually goes back to the OR.1

#Orders and tests

  • Who may order what alone varies by site. Ask your senior early.
  • Stat BMP (sodium, glucose, creatinine) and serum osmolality.1,2
  • CBC: blood loss, hemolysis.1
  • Cardiac monitor varies by site. 12-lead ECG now.3,7
  • Chest X-ray if short of breath, crackles or low SpO2.4,5
  • Low SpO2: oxygen, and tell your senior now varies by site.4
  • Repeat sodium often. Senior sets the interval varies by site.2
  • CBI: normal saline only.1
  • Strict I/O, neuro checks varies by site.
  • Fall and seizure precautions varies by site. Low sodium can cause both.2
  • Suspect perforation: cystogram, ordered with your senior.1
  • If the page comes from the OR or PACU, the surgeon and anesthesia lead. Tell them now varies by site.
  • Hypertonic saline or a diuretic: only with your senior. Senior picks the drug, dose and rate varies by site.1,2
  • ICU transfer: senior decides varies by site.

#Call the senior

Situation
Urology, about bed 12, Mr. X, 2 hours after monopolar TURP. New confusion, nausea, blurry vision. BP 92/50, HR 54. Rapid response team called.
Background
Long resection with glycine irrigation. Pre-op sodium normal.
Assessment
Concern for TUR syndrome. On a cardiac monitor; ECG done. Lungs clear, SpO2 96% on room air. Belly soft, no shoulder pain, perforation not ruled out. CBI switched to saline. Stat sodium and osmolality sent.
Request
Please come now. Need the sodium plan, level of care, and whether to hold the CBI.

#Follow up

  • Recheck sodium at the time your senior sets varies by site.2
  • Watch for too fast a rise. Senior sets the limit varies by site.2
  • Nurse pages for new confusion, seizure, vision change, low BP, slow pulse, low SpO2 or fast breathing.1,4,6
  • Nurse pages for a swollen belly or a bag that stops draining.
  • Document: time of page, symptoms, vitals, irrigation fluid, sodium values with times.
  • Document who you told and the plan.
  • Hand off: sodium trend, next draw time, irrigation fluid, treatment running.
  • Hand off what to do if he changes overnight.

#Normal or warning sign

Typical at many centers:

Expected
After bipolar resection with saline, TUR syndrome is rare.1
Expected
Post-op CBI runs on normal saline.1
Not expected
New confusion, nausea, or vision change after a monopolar case.1
Not expected
Low BP or shock after a monopolar case. Think TUR syndrome or perforation.1,3
Not expected
Glycine, sorbitol, or mannitol running on the floor.1
Note
Bipolar has its own risks: fluid overload, hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis.1

#Avoid

  • Don't leave hypotonic irrigation running after the resection. Switch to normal saline. If he is bleeding, keep the saline CBI on: fresh clots can block the catheter. Perforation concern: ask your senior about holding the CBI varies by site.1,8
  • Don't correct sodium on your own. Too fast a rise can cause osmotic demyelination, mainly in chronic low sodium. Acute TUR syndrome with brain symptoms: treat without delay. Waiting risks brain swelling.2
  • Don't miss perforation. Swollen belly, low BP, slow pulse, belly pain: immediate cystogram, with your senior.1

#Study questions

Why does monopolar TURP cause low sodium?

Monopolar needs a non-conducting fluid such as glycine, sorbitol or mannitol.1

The body absorbs it, and it dilutes the sodium.1

Who is at high risk?

Low sodium before surgery, kidney failure, high irrigation pressure, long resection.1

A missed perforation or heavy bleeding.1

When should sodium be checked after a monopolar TURP?

Check it if: resection over 60 minutes, or low pre-op sodium.1

Failed outflow drainage during the case, or heavy bleeding.1

Severe cases are unlikely at 90 minutes or less.1

At what sodium do symptoms start?

Usually at 125 mEq/L or lower, and likely at 120.1

A fast fall can cause symptoms too. The level alone can mislead.2

Low BP and HR 54. TUR syndrome or perforation?

Either. TUR syndrome can cause low BP, slow pulse or arrhythmia.3

Perforation: low BP, slow pulse, swollen belly, belly or shoulder pain.1

A missed perforation raises TUR syndrome risk too.1

Why send osmolality too?

It sorts the type of low sodium.2

The chapter lists TUR syndrome as isotonic low sodium: osmolality near normal.2

A gap between measured and calculated osmolality: unmeasured solutes, such as sugars.2

Glycine breaks down to ammonia, which can make him worse.2

How is the risk lowered?

Bipolar resection with saline irrigation nearly removes it.1

Monopolar: irrigation low, about 60 cm above the table.1

Short resection.1

How is it treated?

The two sources differ. Senior picks the plan varies by site.

TURP chapter, mild to moderate: furosemide with normal saline, slow correction.1

TURP chapter, severe or symptomatic: hypertonic saline, with care.1

Hyponatremia chapter, milder symptoms: slow hypertonic saline.2

Hyponatremia chapter, severe symptoms: hypertonic saline.2

Severe low sodium: consult nephrology, with your senior varies by site.2
