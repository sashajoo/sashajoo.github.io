Ileus after cystectomy

Common callSasha ZhuReviewed, version 0.1Last checked 2026-10-11Pocket card

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, POD 4 after radical cystectomy with ileal conduit. Nausea, vomited twice, belly distended, no flatus.

Abbreviations: RN, registered nurse; POD, postoperative day; WBC, white blood cell count; BMP, basic metabolic panel; CBC, complete blood count; NG, nasogastric; CT, computed tomography; IV, intravenous; I/O, intake and output; HR, heart rate; BP, blood pressure; TPN, total parenteral nutrition; ERAS, enhanced recovery after surgery.

#Danger signs

  • Fever, fast pulse, focal tenderness, high WBC or acidosis. Think strangulated bowel. Surgery may be needed.1
  • Rigid belly, rebound or guarding. Think peritonitis or perforation.1,2
  • High lactate: think ischemic bowel.1
  • Fever after a urine leak: think infected urinoma.3

#First steps

  1. See him. Vitals, then belly: distension, tympany, tenderness, rebound.2,4
  2. Ask: did flatus or stool come, then stop? What does the vomit look like?4
  3. Check every tube. Drain output, stoma or catheter urine output, and the trend.5,6
  4. Nothing by mouth for now varies by site. Send BMP, magnesium, CBC.4,7
  5. Call your senior. Plan NG decompression and imaging together.4,8

#Key decision

#Do not miss

  1. Mechanical obstruction or ischemic bowel. Call it ileus only once these are ruled out.2,4
  2. Leak: bowel anastomosis or ureter-to-bowel connection. Urine leak can cause chemical peritonitis and ileus.3,10
  3. Abscess or sepsis. Aspiration from ongoing vomiting.2,4

#Orders and tests

  • Who may order what alone varies by site. Ask your senior early.
  • Labs: BMP (potassium, chloride, bicarbonate, creatinine), magnesium, CBC.1,4,7
  • Low potassium can itself cause nausea, vomiting, ileus and distension. Replace with your senior varies by site.4,7
  • Low magnesium often comes with low potassium. Correct both, with your senior. Potassium alone may not work.7
  • Looks ill: lactate, blood gas, blood cultures.1,9
  • Imaging: abdominal X-rays first. Ileus: air in colon and rectum, no transition point.4
  • CT abdomen and pelvis with your senior if the X-rays are unclear or strangulation is a concern.1,4
  • Contrast choice: senior and radiology varies by site.4
  • Drains: send drain fluid for creatinine. Compare with serum creatinine.6
  • NG tube to suction if vomiting or very distended, placed with your senior varies by site.4,8
  • IV fluids: crystalloid first; your senior sets the volume.1,2,9
  • Strict I/O, including NG, drain and stoma output.5
  • Pain: non-opioid options, with your senior. Opioids slow the gut.2
  • Walk him if safe.2,4

#Call the senior

Situation
Urology, about bed 12, Mr. X, POD 4 after cystectomy with ileal conduit. Vomiting and distended.
Background
No flatus yet. Drain output up since this morning.
Assessment
Afebrile, HR 92, BP 128/76, no rebound. Past 72 h, so look for a cause.
Request
Please see him. NG tube, imaging and drain creatinine?

#Follow up

  • Serial exams and vitals. Senior sets the interval varies by site.1,2
  • Recheck potassium during replacement. Senior sets the timing varies by site.7
  • Vomiting lowers potassium and causes alkalosis. Heavy NG irrigation can lower potassium too.1,8
  • Nurse pages for fever, rising HR, new focal pain, feculent vomit, or rising drain output.1,4,6
  • Not better after several days, or worse on supportive care: more imaging.2
  • No adequate oral intake by 7 days: TPN is discussed.4
  • Document: time of page, exam, I/O, labs, imaging, who you told, the plan.
  • Hand off: NG output, potassium trend, drain creatinine result, what to do if it changes overnight.

#Normal or warning sign

Typical at many centers:

Common
Prolonged ileus in about 20% after cystectomy with diversion.3,10
Expected
ERAS pathways in the US: walking and diet start on POD 0 to 1.11
Note
After cystectomy, first stool can take about 4 days, even with ERAS (some studies).11
Not expected
Bowel function came back, then stopped, with vomiting and pain.4
Not expected
Feculent vomit, intense pain, or fast-growing distension.4
Not expected
Rebound, focal tenderness, fever, or rising drain output.1,2,6

#Avoid

  • Don't call it "just ileus" before obstruction, leak and abscess are ruled out.2,3,4
  • Don't check NG position by pushing air and listening. Aspirate bilious fluid, or get an X-ray.8
  • Don't treat pain with opioids alone. Ask your senior about non-opioid options.2,11

#Study questions

How do you tell ileus from small bowel obstruction?

Ileus: slow onset, no return of bowel function, quiet belly. Obstruction: function returned then stopped, sudden pain, feculent vomit, transition point on imaging.2,4

Why can a urine leak cause ileus after cystectomy?

Urine from the ureter-to-bowel connection irritates the peritoneum. The chemical peritonitis can prolong ileus. Drain fluid creatinine far above serum suggests urine.3,6

Does every patient need an NG tube after surgery?

No. Routine NG after abdominal surgery is not recommended. In one review of ERAS for cystectomy, skipping the NG tube was linked to shorter stay.8,11

What does ERAS add for the bowel after cystectomy?

Early walking, early feeding, goal-directed fluids, multimodal pain control. Many pathways add a mu-opioid antagonist such as alvimopan. A 2023 meta-analysis pooled 25 studies. In the 17 that reported ileus, ERAS lowered it (odds ratio 0.61).5,11,12

Do prokinetics or laxatives help once ileus has started?

StatPearls Ileus chapter: not effective once ileus is diagnosed (Level 1). Gum chewing may help.4
