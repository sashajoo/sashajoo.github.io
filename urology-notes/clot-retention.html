Hematuria and clot retention

Common callSasha ZhuReviewed, version 0.1Last checked 2026-10-11Pocket card

Page

RN: Mr. X, POD 0 after TURP, on CBI. Urine is dark red, the bag stopped filling, and he has bad lower belly pain.

Abbreviations: RN, registered nurse; POD, postoperative day; TURP, transurethral resection of the prostate; CBI, continuous bladder irrigation; HR, heart rate; BP, blood pressure; CBC, complete blood count; Hgb, hemoglobin; BMP, basic metabolic panel; OR, operating room; TUR, transurethral resection; PT/INR, prothrombin time / international normalized ratio; PTT, partial thromboplastin time; IV, intravenous; I/O, intake and output; Fr, French, the catheter size unit; CT, computed tomography.

#Danger signs

  • HR up, BP down, Hgb falling, cold hands or feet. Tachycardia often comes first.1,2
  • Tense belly or shoulder pain, with low BP and slow pulse.3
  • New confusion or nausea, or fever.2,3

#First steps

  1. Stop the CBI inflow now. Fluid is going in but not coming out.4,5
  2. See him: vitals, a full tender bladder, kinks or closed clamps on the line.2,4,5
  3. Read the op note first. Known or suspected perforation: call your senior before any irrigation varies by site.3,6
  4. Hand-irrigate: catheter-tip syringe and saline, push in and pull back until the return is clear varies by site.4,7
  5. Send CBC, BMP, PT/INR and PTT. Type and screen varies by site. Call your senior.2,7

#Key decision

#Do not miss

  1. Hemorrhage with shock. Severe bleeding after surgery usually goes back to the OR.3
  2. Overdistended or perforated bladder. Intraperitoneal tears usually need surgery.3,5,7,11
  3. TUR syndrome (low sodium) after monopolar TURP. Rare with bipolar and saline.3

#Orders and tests

  • Who may order what alone varies by site. Ask your senior early.
  • Labs: CBC, BMP (sodium, creatinine), PT/INR, PTT. Type and screen varies by site.2,7
  • Sodium now if he is confused or nauseated, or after a monopolar case over 60 min or with heavy bleeding.3
  • Symptoms usually start at sodium 125 mEq/L or lower.3
  • IV fluids if HR is up or BP is down. Your senior sets the volume.2
  • Still bleeding: blood products early. Avoid large crystalloid volumes. Crossmatch and transfusion with your senior varies by site.1,7
  • CBI: once the return is clear, restart fast, then slow it to light pink varies by site.3,8
  • Strict I/O. True urine output = bag volume minus irrigation volume.4
  • He already has a 3-way catheter after TURP. Hand irrigation fails: new large 3-way (22 Fr or more), with your senior.3,4,7
  • Then wash out again through the new catheter.4
  • Ask your senior to check the catheter position. After TURP the balloon can slip into the resected prostate cavity (the fossa).3,10
  • Bleeding from the prostate cavity: your senior may add water to the balloon or put the catheter on traction. Traction pulls the balloon against the bladder neck.3,9,10
  • Traction is kept short. Too long can injure the bladder neck and narrow it later.9
  • Anticoagulants: stop or reverse with your senior varies by site.2,7
  • Antiplatelets: ask your senior varies by site.12,13,14,15
  • Suspect perforation: CT cystogram with retrograde filling, ordered with your senior. Clamping the Foley is not enough.3,11
  • Fever with low BP (shock or sepsis likely): blood and urine cultures, then broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour.2,16
  • Low BP with sepsis: crystalloid first. Your senior sets the volume.16
  • Not blocked yet (gross hematuria, still draining): urinalysis, urine culture, CBC, BMP, PT/INR, PTT.2
  • Clots or trouble voiding: large 3-way catheter and CBI.7
  • Unexplained gross hematuria needs cystoscopy and CT urogram later.7

#Call the senior

Situation
Urology, Mr. X in bed 12, clot retention.
Background
POD 0 after TURP, on CBI, no perforation in the op note.
Assessment
Bag stopped filling, belly pain, full bladder, HR 112, BP 104/68. CBI stopped; hand irrigation does not clear it.
Request
Please come now to change the catheter. Labs are sent.

#Follow up

  • Recheck after the fix: bag filling, color lighter, belly soft, vitals OK. Set a recheck time with the nurse varies by site.
  • Nurse pages if the bag stops again, the color gets darker, BP drops or HR rises.4
  • Document: time of page, vitals, exam, clot removed, irrigation in and out, labs, who was told, the plan.
  • Hand off: catheter size and type, CBI color goal, Hgb trend, blood thinner status, what to do if it changes overnight.

#Normal or warning sign

Typical at many centers:

Expected
The night after TURP, CBI keeps the bag light pink. It is often stopped the next morning to see if the urine stays light.3
Expected
One hand irrigation clears a few clots, and the bag keeps filling.4
Not expected
The bag stays dark red with CBI at full flow, or hand irrigation is needed again and again.4
Not expected
HR up, BP down, Hgb falling, a swollen belly, or new confusion.1,3

#Avoid

  • Don't let CBI keep running into a catheter that does not drain. The bladder overfills.4,5
  • Don't force or blindly swap the catheter after TURP on your own. A standard tip can make a false passage in the resected prostate.17
  • Don't leave clot in the bladder. It blocks the catheter and keeps the bleeding going.7

#Study questions

Why get all the clot out?

Clot blocks the catheter. As it breaks down, it acts like a blood thinner. Bleeding goes on.7

Hand irrigation does not clear it. What next?

Call the senior. Next: a new large 3-way catheter (22 Fr or more). If that fails: clot evacuation and fulguration in the OR.3,7

The bag clears, then bright red bleeding comes back. What can your senior do at the bedside?

Check the catheter position: the balloon can slip into the resected cavity. Put the catheter on traction, so the balloon presses on the bladder neck. Keep it short, to avoid bladder neck injury. Still bleeding: the OR.3,7,9,10

What predicts a procedure in gross hematuria?

One single-center cohort in Jordan: 330 adults admitted with gross hematuria, not post-TURP. 30% needed a procedure. Clot retention was the strongest predictor (odds ratio 4.11).18
