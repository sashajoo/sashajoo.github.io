Emergency

Infected obstructing stone

Page

ED: Mr. X, 58, left ureteral stone on CT, temp 39.0 °C, HR 118, BP 98/60. Can urology see him?

Danger signs

  • Stone plus any of these: think infected, blocked kidney.
    • Fever 37.8 °C (100 °F) or higher
    • WBC over 15,000
    • Pyuria
    • Low BP
    • Unexplained fast heart rate
  • Systolic BP under 90 or new confusion: likely sepsis, possible shock. Antibiotics within 1 hour.
  • He can look stable, then become critically ill within hours.
    • Higher risk: older age, diabetes, solitary kidney, immunocompromised.

Key decision

Ifblocked kidney plus signs of infection → urgent drainage (stent or nephrostomy tube) plus antibiotics.
Iftoo unstable for general anesthesia, or the blockage is severe → nephrostomy tube often preferred varies by site.
Ifblood thinners cannot be stopped, or severe obesity → a ureteral stent may be needed.

Call the senior

S
Urology, ED consult, Mr. X, 58, left proximal ureteral stone with fever.
R
Please see him now for urgent drainage. Stent, or IR nephrostomy tube? Also, what pain medicine do you want? NSAIDs are not safe for him.
