Patient safety habits

Eight habits that run through every call.

Call your senior

See the patient first. Have the chart open. Then talk in SBAR.

S · Situation
Urology team, about bed 14, POD 1 TURP. Urine dark red with clots for 1 hour.
B · Background
On bladder irrigation. Irrigation turned up and hand-irrigated, still dark red. HR 112, BP 98/60. Hgb 11.2 today, 13.0 before surgery.
A · Assessment
I think he is bleeding. The catheter may clot off.
R · Request
Please come and see him now. I have sent a CBC and type and screen.

Have ready: vitals, labs with times, meds, allergies, code status. Example, not a real patient.

More

Source: IHI SBAR Tool; TeamSTEPPS® 3.0 Pocket Guide (AHRQ and DoD, 2023)

Hand off with I-PASS

Face to face, few interruptions. The receiver says it back.

More

Source: TeamSTEPPS® 3.0 Pocket Guide (AHRQ and DoD, 2023); Starmer, Pediatrics 2012; Starmer, NEJM 2014; Joint Commission, Sentinel Event Alert 58 (2017)

Closed-loop communication

Say it, repeat it, confirm it. Then act.

  1. Call-outSenior: "Sam, send a type and screen and a repeat Hgb now."
  2. Check-backSam: "Type and screen and repeat Hgb, now."
  3. ConfirmSenior: "Correct."

Call-out names the one person who does the task. Check-back shows the message was understood.

More

Source: TeamSTEPPS® 3.0 Pocket Guide (AHRQ and DoD, 2023)

Speak up

Any team member can stop the work for a safety problem.

  1. C"I am Concerned."
  2. U"I am Uncomfortable."
  3. S"This is a Safety issue." Stop the work.
More

Two-challenge rule

  1. Concern ignored: say it again
  2. They must say they heard and understood it
  3. Still unsafe: say the danger another way
  4. Still unsafe: take stronger action. More team members, supervisor, chain of command

Source: TeamSTEPPS® 3.0 Pocket Guide (AHRQ and DoD, 2023)

Escalate early

Warning signs often come hours before harm. Call while there is time.

  1. Unstable, or meets a call criterionRapid response team. Any staff member may call.
  2. Getting worseSenior now. Senior not reachable: call the attending.
  3. WorriedSenior, in SBAR. Very worried: you may call the rapid response team yourself.
More

Typical call criteria (AHRQ PSNet example; follow your hospital's list)

  • Staff member has significant concern
  • HR over 140 or under 40
  • RR over 28 or under 8
  • SBP over 180 or under 90
  • SpO2 under 90% on oxygen
  • Acute change in mental status
  • Urine output under 50 mL in 4 hours

Source: AHRQ PSNet, Rapid Response Systems (2024)

Keep a to-do list

Each task has an owner and a time. Open tasks get handed off.

  1. WriteWhat · who owns it · by when
  2. Re-checkAt huddles and before sign-out: done, or still open?
  3. Hand offStill open at shift end: goes on the I-PASS action list
More

Source: TeamSTEPPS® 3.0 Pocket Guide (AHRQ and DoD, 2023); Joint Commission, Sentinel Event Alert 58 (2017)

Scan with STEP

Look past the one task. Check the whole situation, again and again.

S · Status of the patient
Vitals, exam, meds, plan, patient's concerns
T · Team members
Fatigue, workload, stress, skill
E · Environment
Equipment, staffing, how sick the unit is
P · Progress toward the goal
Tasks done? Is the plan still right?
More

Source: TeamSTEPPS® 3.0 Pocket Guide (AHRQ and DoD, 2023)

Check yourself

Are you safe to work right now? Watch each other too.

  • I · Illness
  • M · Medication
  • S · Stress
  • A · Alcohol and drugs
  • F · Fatigue
  • E · Eating and elimination

Cross-monitoring: watch each other's work, so a mistake is caught fast.
