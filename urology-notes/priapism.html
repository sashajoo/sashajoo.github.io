Priapism

EmergencySasha ZhuReviewed, version 0.1Last checked 2026-10-11Pocket card

Page

ED: Mr. X, 24, sickle cell disease, painful erection since 3 am, now 6 hours. Can urology see him?

Abbreviations: ED, emergency department; OR, operating room; BP, blood pressure; CBC, complete blood count; pO2, partial pressure of oxygen; pCO2, partial pressure of carbon dioxide.

#Danger signs

  • Rigid, tender erection for 4 hours or more, soft glans: think ischemic priapism. A true emergency.1

#First steps

  1. Get the exact start time. Duration predicts outcome best.1
  2. Ask: pain, prior episodes, sickle cell disease, trauma to penis or perineum.1
  3. Drugs: erectile dysfunction pills or penile injections, trazodone, antipsychotics, cocaine.1
  4. Exam: rigidity, tenderness, glans, pulsation. Perineum and abdomen: any bruising?1
  5. Looks ischemic: page your senior now. No imaging first. Do not delay treatment for labs.1,2

#Key decision

#Do not miss

  1. Ischemic priapism treated late: fibrosis, permanent erectile dysfunction. Urology treats it now, with your senior.1,2
  2. Sickle cell disease: same penile treatment, not fluids alone. Plus hydration, oxygen, pain control, hematology.1,3
  3. Phenylephrine side effects: high BP, slow or fast heart rate, arrhythmia. Monitor throughout.1,2

#Orders and tests

  • CBC and reticulocyte count.1
  • Sickle cell test (hemoglobin S) if at any risk.1
  • Type and screen in sickle cell disease.1
  • Urine toxicology and blood alcohol, if relevant.1
  • Corporal blood gas: drawn at aspiration by the urology team, with your senior.2
  • Some centers: trained ED physicians do the aspiration varies by site.2
  • Color duplex Doppler if the type is unclear. Only if available without delay varies by site.1
  • Pain control, per your senior varies by site.2
  • Ask pharmacy early for diluted phenylephrine. Your senior sets the strength varies by site.2
  • Procedure kit to bedside: block, prep, blood gas syringe, saline, butterfly needles, syringes.2
  • Cardiac monitor and BP every 5 to 10 minutes once phenylephrine starts.1,2

#Call the senior

Situation
Urology, ED consult, Mr. X in bed 12, painful erection for 6 hours.
Background
Sickle cell disease, no trauma, no erectile dysfunction drugs.
Assessment
Rigid, tender shaft, soft glans. Looks ischemic. CBC, reticulocyte count, type and screen sent.
Request
Please come now for aspiration and phenylephrine.

#Follow up

  • After the erection goes down: watch for recurrence and phenylephrine side effects.2
  • Pressure on puncture sites for 1 minute. Gauze and a self-adherent wrap.2
  • Remove the cause if possible, such as a drug. Do it with the prescriber varies by site.2
  • Teach: if the erection comes back, return right away.1,2
  • Urology follow-up for every patient. Recurrent episodes: screen for sickle cell disease.1,2
  • Document condition and treatment. Write down that you discussed the risk of erectile dysfunction.1
  • Hand off: duration, what was done, response, plan if it recurs.

#Normal or warning sign

Typical at many centers:

Expected
Aspiration alone often fails. Irrigation turns dark blood bright red after several rounds.1,2
Expected
Pain during the procedure, even with a good block.2
Possible
Penile hematoma at the puncture site. A pressure dressing reduces it.2
Not expected
Headache, palpitations, high BP or arrhythmia on phenylephrine. Tell your senior now.1,2
Not expected
Erection comes back. Call your senior. More treatment or a shunt may follow.2

#Avoid

  • Don't inject phenylephrine or shunt for non-ischemic priapism. It does not work.1
  • Don't rely on oral drugs alone, or wait for them to work. Terbutaline is not recommended.1,2
  • Don't give phenylephrine without a cardiac monitor and frequent BP checks.1,2

#Study questions

How do you tell ischemic from non-ischemic priapism?

Ischemic: painful, fully rigid, soft glans.1

Non-ischemic: painless, partly rigid, often after perineal trauma.1

If unclear: corporal blood gas or color duplex Doppler.1

What are the common causes?

Ischemic: penile injections for erectile dysfunction, trazodone, antipsychotics, cocaine.1

Sickle cell disease and blood cancers too.1

Non-ischemic: trauma that makes an artery-to-corpus fistula.1

Why does time matter so much?

Damage starts at about 6 hours. Permanent structural change after 14 hours.1

Past 24 hours, up to 90% lose normal erections.1

Lasting damage begins within 36 hours. Past 72 hours, function is lost.1,2,3,5,6

He has sickle cell disease. What changes?

Same penile treatment. Treat the sickle cell disease at the same time.1

No response: exchange transfusion is considered.1

Why phenylephrine?

It works and has fewer heart side effects than other agents.1,2

It works better after irrigation brings oxygen back.1,2

He presents after 3 days. What now?

Normal erections will likely not recover.2

Your senior discusses early penile prosthesis with him.2
