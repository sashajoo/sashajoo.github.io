Hypotension on the night of surgery

Page

RN: Mr. H., bed 4, POD 0 after open left nephrectomy, epidural running. BP 84/50, HR 112. He feels dizzy.

Danger signs

  • SBP under 90 or MAP under 65.
  • More serious with confusion, cold clammy skin or low urine output.
  • Rising HR, falling Hgb, or more or darker drain blood.
  • New abdominal, flank or back pain.
  • Fever, chest pain, dyspnea, low SpO2, or urticaria and wheeze.
  • Low BP, low SpO2 and reduced or absent breath sounds on one side: consider tension pneumothorax.

Key decision

Ifcold skin, rising HR, falling Hgb or more drain blood → treat as bleeding. Senior now, and get blood ready
Iffever or warm skin → sepsis. Send cultures. Antibiotics within 1 hour if shock is possible or sepsis is likely
Ifurticaria or wheeze after a new drug or blood → anaphylaxis. Stop the drug or blood, give IM epinephrine and call rapid response
Iflow SpO2, dyspnea and reduced or absent breath sounds on one side → possible tension pneumothorax. Call rapid response and your senior now. Do not wait for an X-ray
