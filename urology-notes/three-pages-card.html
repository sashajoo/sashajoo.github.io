Common call

Three pages at once

Page

RN, three pages in 5 minutes: bed 12, POD 1 after nephrectomy, temp 38.4 °C. Bed 7, 81, wants something to help him sleep. Bed 3, Foley not draining for 2 hours, lower belly pain.

Danger signs

  • Vitals past typical rapid response triggers varies by site:
    • HR over 140 or under 40. RR over 28 or under 8.
    • Systolic BP over 180 or under 90. SpO2 under 90% on oxygen.
  • New confusion or a sudden change in mental status.
  • New delirium can signal a life-threatening illness.
  • The nurse is worried. Staff concern alone is a rapid response trigger.

Key decision

Ifanyone meets a rapid response trigger, or the nurse is very worried → go there first. Call the RRT or your senior now.
Ifbed 7 is newly confused → new confusion is a danger sign. See bed 7 now.
Iftwo or more may be sick → call your senior now. Agree on who sees whom.
Ifall vitals are normal, no one newly confused → see bed 3 first: blocked Foley with pain. Phone the nurses for the other two.

Call the senior

S
Urology, three pages at once. Bed 3 is the main worry.
B
Foley not draining for 2 hours, lower belly pain, full bladder. Vitals normal. Flushing did not clear it.
A
Blocked catheter, maybe clot. Bed 12: 38.4 °C on POD 1, normal vitals. Bed 7 wants a sleep aid, not confused.
R
Please come to bed 3 now, or say which patient to see first.

RN, registered nurse; POD, postoperative day; SpO2, oxygen saturation; RRT, rapid response team; HR, heart rate; RR, respiratory rate; BP, blood pressure.
