Urinary retention consult from the ED, then the voiding trial

Common callSasha ZhuReviewed, version 0.1Last checked 2026-10-11Pocket card

Page

ED: Mr. X, 78, unable to urinate since last night, lower belly pain, bladder scan about 900 mL. Can urology see him?

Abbreviations: ED, emergency department; BP, blood pressure; MRI, magnetic resonance imaging; BPH, benign prostatic hyperplasia; AKI, acute kidney injury; Fr, French, the catheter size unit; CBI, continuous bladder irrigation; BMP, basic metabolic panel; BUN, blood urea nitrogen; CBC, complete blood count; PVR, post-void residual.

#Danger signs

  • Retention plus fever, confusion, or low BP that does not recover: possible urosepsis. Start the sepsis workup now.1,2
  • New back pain, saddle numbness, leg weakness or bowel change: think cauda equina. Urgent MRI and spine team.3,4

#First steps

  1. Ask the ED: how long, fever, blood or clots, back pain or numbness. Past prostate or urethral surgery? Catheter tries so far?4,5
  2. Bladder scan if quick. Over 400 mL fits retention varies by site. Obvious case: never delay the Foley.4
  3. Standard Foley: ED nurse or ED doctor varies by site. Won't pass: no force. A coudé is next, only by someone trained in it: an ED doctor or nurse, or urology varies by site. See the page "The Foley that will not go in".5,6
  4. Drain fully, no clamping. Record the volume.4
  5. Look for the cause: BPH, anticholinergic or opioid, constipation (rectal exam), recent surgery or anesthesia.4,7

#Key decision

#Do not miss

  1. Urosepsis: drain, urine culture, call your senior.2,4
    • Broad-spectrum antibiotics: pick them by local resistance data.2
  2. Cauda equina: painless retention can be a late sign. Ask about saddle numbness and bowel change every time.3
  3. Clot retention: retained clots clog the Foley. With your senior: large (22 Fr or bigger) 3-way catheter, hand irrigation, then CBI.9

#Orders and tests

  • Who may order what alone varies by site. Ask your senior early.
  • Foley, free drainage.4
  • Urinalysis. Urine culture from the new Foley if infection is possible.4
  • BMP (sodium, potassium, BUN, creatinine) and CBC.4
  • Renal ultrasound if over 1500 mL drained or creatinine newly high.4
  • Over 1500 mL drained or AKI: urine output every 1 to 2 hours, electrolytes and creatinine about every 12 hours varies by site.8
  • Alpha-blocker such as tamsulosin if no contraindication, with your senior's OK.4
  • Recommend holding drugs that cause retention: anticholinergics, opioids when possible.4
  • Septic and low BP: the ED leads resuscitation varies by site. Recommend crystalloid first; confirm with your senior.2

#Call the senior

Situation
Urology, ED consult, Mr. X, 78, acute retention.
Background
Foley passed easily, 900 mL clear urine. No fever, no back or nerve signs, creatinine at baseline.
Assessment
Likely BPH. No sign of sepsis, AKI or cauda equina.
Request
Start tamsulosin, home with the Foley, voiding trial in about 3 days. OK, or do you want to see him?4

#Follow up

  • Recheck urine output in the first hours. Watch for post-obstructive diuresis if over 1500 mL drained, AKI or heart failure.4
  • Post-obstructive diuresis: 200 mL/h or more for 2 hours in a row, or over 3 L in 24 hours.8
  • Physiologic at first. Follow it closely. Call your senior; replace only part of the output, per their plan.8
  • Admit (discuss with your senior): urosepsis, AKI, electrolyte problem, over 1500 mL drained, cancer obstruction, acute myelopathy varies by site.4
  • Document right away: volume drained. Catheter type and size, who placed it, number of tries varies by site.4,5
  • Most go home with the Foley. Teach him to use a leg bag by day and a night bag. No prophylactic antibiotics.4
  • Voiding trial in about 3 days, after the alpha-blocker has worked varies by site. Big volume or long retention: Foley stays longer.4
  • Drug or anesthesia cause: trial can be sooner, hours to 3 days varies by site.4
  • Trial: Foley out in the morning, or fill the bladder through the Foley first, per local protocol. Void, then check PVR. PVR under 200 mL: pass. Cannot void or over 400 mL: fail. 200 to 400 mL: decide with the patient varies by site.4
  • Failed trial: catheter again, Foley or self-catheterization varies by site. Outpatient urology workup.4,7

#Normal or warning sign

Typical at many centers:

Expected
Mild blood in the urine after drainage. Usually stops on its own or with simple irrigation.4
Expected
Brief BP drop right after drainage. Usually recovers without treatment.4
Expected
After draining a large volume: physiologic diuresis for up to about a day. Follow the output.8
Not expected
Diuresis that goes on, or comes with dizziness, low BP that does not recover, or confusion. Think pathologic post-obstructive diuresis. Call your senior.8
Not expected
Clots, or the Foley stops draining. Think clot retention. Call your senior.9

#Avoid

  • Don't clamp the catheter or drain in steps. Neither prevents bleeding, low BP or diuresis.4,8
  • Don't force a catheter.5,6
  • Don't inflate the balloon until the catheter is all the way in to the hub.5
  • Blood at the meatus: no blind attempt.5,6
  • No infection: no antibiotics just for the Foley.4

#Study questions

Why start the alpha-blocker before the voiding trial, and why wait about 3 days?

It relaxes prostate and bladder neck smooth muscle, so the trial is more likely to pass. Full effect takes about 3 days, so start it when the Foley goes in.4

He drained 2 liters. What is the worry now?

Post-obstructive diuresis, AKI, and a bladder that may not recover well. Check creatinine and electrolytes. Follow urine output closely. Discuss admission with your senior. Leave the Foley in longer before the trial.4,8
