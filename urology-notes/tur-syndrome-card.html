Low sodium after TURP

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, 2 hours after monopolar TURP. New confusion, nausea, says his vision is blurry. BP 92/50, HR 54.

Danger signs

  • Seizure, very drowsy, coma, or shock: severe low sodium.
  • New confusion or vision change after a monopolar case. Even nausea alone: stat sodium.
  • Low BP and slow pulse: TUR syndrome or bladder perforation. Check for both.
  • Short of breath, crackles or low SpO2: fluid overload with lung edema.

Key decision

Iflow sodium with seizure, very drowsy, or delirium → senior at bedside now. Hypertonic saline per senior varies by site
Ifmilder symptoms only (nausea, tired), fully alert → senior picks: furosemide with saline, or slow hypertonic saline varies by site
Ifswollen belly, belly pain or shoulder pain → think perforation, at any sodium. Cystogram with your senior

Call the senior

S
Urology, about bed 12, Mr. X, 2 hours after monopolar TURP. New confusion, nausea, blurry vision. BP 92/50, HR 54. Rapid response team called.
B
Long resection with glycine irrigation. Pre-op sodium normal.
A
Concern for TUR syndrome. On a cardiac monitor; ECG done. Lungs clear, SpO2 96% on room air. Belly soft, no shoulder pain, perforation not ruled out. CBI switched to saline. Stat sodium and osmolality sent.
R
Please come now. Need the sodium plan, level of care, and whether to hold the CBI.
