Testicular torsion

EmergencySasha ZhuReviewed, version 0.1Last checked 2026-10-11Pocket card

Page

ED: Mr. X, 19, sudden left testicle pain for 3 hours, vomited twice. Can urology see him?

Abbreviations: ED, emergency department; NPO, nothing by mouth; OR, operating room; CBC, complete blood count; CT, computed tomography; TWIST, Testicular Workup for Ischemia and Suspected Torsion; MRI, magnetic resonance imaging.

#Danger signs

  • Sudden one-sided scrotal pain, sometimes with nausea or vomiting: think torsion.2
  • Torsion is a true emergency. The chance of saving the testis drops after 6 hours of pain.2
  • Pain only in the lower belly or groin can still be torsion. Examine the scrotum.2

#First steps

  1. Get the exact time the pain started.2,3
  2. Examine both sides: lie, height, swelling, hardness, cremasteric reflex.2,3
  3. High suspicion: call your senior now. Don't wait for ultrasound.2,3
  4. Keep him NPO. Prepare him as if for the OR.2
  5. Unsure: urgent Doppler ultrasound, only if it does not delay your senior's review or the OR.2,3

#Key decision

#Do not miss

  • Testicular torsion: time-dependent, the most important cause of testis loss.2
  • Incarcerated or strangulated inguinal hernia: can cut off blood flow.3
  • Fournier gangrene: feel the scrotum, perineum and thighs for crepitus.3

#Orders and tests

  • NPO.2
  • Pre-op blood work varies by site.2
  • Urgent scrotal Doppler ultrasound, both sides, only if it does not delay the OR.2,3
  • Urinalysis. Pyuria does not rule out torsion.2
  • CBC, urine culture, gonorrhea and chlamydia testing, if no delay.3
  • Septic patient: blood cultures and cross-sectional imaging, with your senior.3
  • Booking the OR and consent: with your senior varies by site.

#Call the senior

Situation
Urology consult, ED bed 12, Mr. X, 19, possible left torsion.
Background
Sudden left scrotal pain 3 hours ago, vomited twice.
Assessment
Left testis high and hard, no cremasteric reflex. High suspicion.
Request
Please see him now. He is NPO, labs sent.

#Follow up

  • Document: time pain started, time seen, time senior called, time to OR.2
  • Normal ultrasound but strong exam: tell your senior. Ultrasound can miss torsion.2
  • After manual detorsion: recheck pain. He still goes to the OR.3
  • Hand off: onset time, exam, OR plan, who was told.
  • Surgery: explore, untwist, fix the testis. Other side fixed too.2,3
  • Testis removed: a prosthesis may be offered later (one source: 4 to 6 months).2
  • Torsion can recur after orchiopexy.2
  • No sports until the urologist clears him.3

#Normal or warning sign

Typical at many centers:

Expected
Near-instant pain relief after a successful manual detorsion.3
Possible
A cremasteric reflex in some proven torsions (8 to 30% in case series).3
Not expected
Pain gets worse during detorsion. Try the other direction, with your senior.2
Not expected
Crepitus or a septic patient. Think Fournier gangrene or abscess. Senior now. CT varies by site.3

#Avoid

  • Don't delay the OR for ultrasound when suspicion is high.2,3
  • Don't rule out torsion by a cremasteric reflex, the Prehn sign or pyuria.2,3
  • Don't skip the OR after a successful manual detorsion.3

#Study questions

Pain started 14 hours ago. Is the OR still worth it?

Yes, explore without delay even past 6 hours. In one systematic review, 61% of testes were still alive at 13 to 18 hours.1,3

How is manual detorsion done?

Medial to lateral, "open the book": right testis counterclockwise, left clockwise, 180 to 720 degrees. Pain relief suggests it worked. He still goes to the OR.2,3

When is manual detorsion used?

Often tried at the bedside, with your senior varies by site. One source uses it only when the OR is delayed. Success 25 to 80%. Another source says not after 6 hours of pain.2,3

What is the TWIST score?

Hard testis 2, swelling 2, nausea or vomiting 1, absent cremasteric reflex 1, high testis 1. 5 or more: high risk, OR without ultrasound. 3 to 4: ultrasound. 2 or less: low risk. Built and tested mostly in children. Used alone, the score missed torsion in about 2 in 100 boys with scrotal pain, so a worrying exam still goes to your senior.2,5,6

How good is ultrasound?

Acute Scrotum Pain chapter: color Doppler 96 to 100% sensitive, 84 to 95% specific. MRI: 93% and 100%. Testicular Torsion chapter: ultrasound 93% sensitive, 100% specific, not perfect in the very young.2,3

How is appendix testis torsion different?

Tenderness near the top of the testis or epididymis, a tender nodule, or a blue dot. More common and not dangerous. Usually no surgery.2

Why fix the other testis?

Bell clapper defect: both sides in at least 2 of 5 cases. Fixing the other side prevents future torsion.2
