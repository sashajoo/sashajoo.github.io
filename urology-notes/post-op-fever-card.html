Common call

Fever after surgery, by day

Page

RN: Mr. X, bed 12, POD 1 after left PCNL. Temp 39.0 °C (102.2 °F), HR 118, shaking chills.

Danger signs

  • Fever with rigors, low BP, fast HR or new confusion: possible sepsis. Act now.
  • Organ dysfunction: SBP under 90, MAP under 65, lactate over 2 mmol/L, low urine output.
    • Any of these with fever: think sepsis.
  • POD 0 to 1, temp over 40 °C (104 °F) with rigidity or acidosis: think malignant hyperthermia.
    • Call anesthesia and your senior now.
  • POD 0 to 1 fever with severe wound pain: think gas gangrene.
  • After PCNL: fever with low SpO2 or short of breath. Think fluid or air in the chest (hydrothorax, pneumothorax), not only sepsis.

Key decision

Iflow BP, possible shock, or sepsis likely → call your senior now. Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour
Ifpossible sepsis, no shock → see him, call your senior. Antibiotics within 3 hours if concern persists
Ifstable, low-grade, first 48 to 72 hours, no source, no stone surgery or surgery on an infected urinary tract → watch, recheck. Do not start antibiotics for the fever alone
